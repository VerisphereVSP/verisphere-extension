Verisphere is in your browser.

Spot misinformation and disinformation as you read — on any site.

1
Select a sentence that makes a claim

Anywhere — a news article, a Reddit comment, a Quora answer, a forwarded post, Wikipedia.

2
Right-click → Check on Verisphere

A side panel shows whether that claim is already on Verisphere and how it stands: how much people have staked that it holds up, and how much that it doesn't. Nothing happens on a page until you ask.

3
Judge for yourself — and weigh in if you want

A claim nobody is willing to back, or that many are challenging, deserves a second look before you believe it or pass it on. You can back or challenge it with VSP of your own, share it with its standing and a link, or, if nobody has put it on Verisphere yet, create the claim from your sentence so others can weigh in.

A Verity Score is a credibility signal, not a verdict: it shows what people are willing to stake behind each side of a claim, which is harder to fake than likes, shares or votes. Use it to notice when something you are reading is contested — or unsupported.

On English Wikipedia the extension also underlines the sentences that already have a claim; hover to see the score, click to open it.

Backing a claim in the panel uses MetaMask on the page. If your wallet is a passkey, Open in the app opens the same claim in Verisphere, where your passkey works — one tap.

Open How to Play for the whole game. The extension reads only from app.verisphere.co and never holds keys or funds; it does nothing on a page until you select text and ask.
